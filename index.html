core4 · Card anatomy & Luhn check

Breaks a Track-2 string into its ISO/IEC 7813 fields and runs the Luhn algorithm — the same checksum every card number carries. Great for showing your wife how "the number" is really several fields glued together. (Runs on whatever you decode, or type one in.)

PAN (card number)— Expiration— Service code— Discretionary— Luhn valid?— Issuer guess—
What do these mean?
PAN — Primary Account Number. First digit = card network (4 Visa, 5 Mastercard, 6 Discover…). Expiration is stored YYMM. Service code (3 digits) tells terminals things like "chip preferred" or "domestic use only". Discretionary data is issuer-defined (may hold a PIN-verification value on some cards). Luhn catches most single-digit typos.

experiment5 · F2F re-modulator (modulate ↔ demodulate)

The heart of "turn data into sound and back." It re-encodes bits into the exact Aiken bi-phase (F2F) waveform a stripe produces: one flux flip per bit clocks a 0; an extra flip in the middle of the bit marks a 1. Play it, watch it, then run it back through the decoder to prove the round-trip.

experiment6 · Parity & LRC playground

Every 5-bit Track-2 character carries an odd-parity bit, and the whole track ends with an LRC column check. Click any data bit to flip it and watch parity turn red and the ASCII change — a hands-on demo of how cards detect read errors. The rightmost column (P) is the parity bit.

experiment7 · Bits → barcode

"See" the stripe: the top strip paints each bit as a black (1) or white (0) column; the bottom strip draws the actual F2F flux-transition pattern — the physical striping on the magnetic tape. Download it as a PNG.

experiment8 · Sonify the stripe

Play the data as music: each bit (or each character) becomes a note, low for 0 and high for 1. Kids love hearing the "song" of a card. Adjust tempo and scale.

experiment9 · DTMF dialer

Turn the card's digits into old-school telephone touch-tones (DTMF). Play the whole number, or poke the keypad. It even listens back to its own tones and prints what it "hears" via Goertzel detection — a mini demo of how phones decode key presses.

experiment10 · Text modem (AFSK)

A general "reimagine any data as sound" toy — a tiny audio modem. It encodes text as two tones (mark/space FSK), like a fax or dial-up handshake. Modulate to hear it, then demodulate the same clip back to text. Feed it your card number, a secret message, anything.
